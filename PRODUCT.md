# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Groups of friends who play pool together, primarily Israeli audience (Hebrew RTL). Context: gathered around a table, phones in hand, settling a rivalry. They open BiliBall right after a game to log the result and check where they stand. They're not serious athletes — they're friends in a group chat that happens to play billiards.

## Product Purpose

BiliBall tracks billiards games within private friend leagues. Win/loss records, leaderboard, head-to-head history, and game notes give every game a paper trail. Success looks like: every group member opens BiliBall after every game, checks the leaderboard, and either celebrates or complains.

## Brand Personality

Competitive, casual, fun. The feeling of a group chat after a big upset. Data is the identity — like FIFA player cards, but for your actual friends. Bold rankings, player cards, team-color energy. Not a serious sports platform. Not a wellness app. A friend-group game that takes its leaderboard seriously.

## Anti-references

Generic fitness trackers — no ring charts, no progress streaks, no motivational banners (Strava / Nike Run Club template). That register implies self-improvement; BiliBall is about rivalry and bragging rights.

## Design Principles

1. **The leaderboard is the hero.** Rankings are the primary emotional surface, not a utility screen. Every design decision should make the leaderboard feel significant.
2. **Rivalry over metrics.** Show the human drama ("Ido beat Yoni") before the raw numbers. Names and narrative come first; stats support them.
3. **Snappy over elaborate.** Pocket-sized group app — every action should feel instant. Minimal friction between "game ended" and "result logged."
4. **Earned specificity.** The billiards aesthetic (balls, felt, pool table) should feel genuinely specific, not clip-art or generic. Use it where it earns its place.
5. **RTL-native.** Hebrew isn't a locale flag — spatial hierarchy, reading flow, and component layout must be designed for right-to-left from the start.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard good practice: readable contrast ratios, generous tap targets (≥44px), RTL layout support. No formal WCAG level required.
